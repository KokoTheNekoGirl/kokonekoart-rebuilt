  - title: Context
    content: |
      During college, we were learning how to code with PHP and touched on the topic of A/B testing. The goal of the project was to create a fun way of displaying two options and having two different outcomes with it. We also had to solve a “real world problem”. I knew exactly what I had to do.

      I've also since converted it to JavaScript to host in the browser insead, and gave a finishing state of the game.

      - src: "@portfolio/lps-real.jpg"

  - title: Inspiration
    content: |
      As a child, I had an LPS Tamagotchi, as well as the bunny in LPS's virtual world when those were huge in the mid-late 2000s. My real world problem was the fact I got rid of my bunny LPS doll, and I'm not the only one to feel a sort of loss for given away toys of our childhoods. I aimed to solve that by creating a pet-care sim in the style of Tamagotchi. Additionally, I thought it would be really cute and nostalgic to replicate the look and feel of the mid-2000s flash games with Tamagotchi-like game play. My stretch goal is to obtain this plush toy again sometime in my life.
    images:
      - src: "@portfolio/lps-inspo.jpg"
        alt: "LPS bunny and virtual world reference. Good old times"

  - title: Process
    content: |
      I first started with asset creation. I kept the bunny on two layers so I could animate it into a GIF. I opted for GIF animations, as it was easier to control the different states with background images.

      The most fun part though was creating overly designed complex buttons. I always love making the most over-the-top button designs. I created them as images as I wasn't confident in my pure-CSS skills yet.

      I also created a cute little simple background to go behind the game.

      Finally, I added some LPS game music, added other little sound effects, and changed the cursor too, all to push that mid 2000s girly flash games feel.
    images:
      - src: "@portfolio/lps-assets.png"
        alt: "All the assets I created for the project, including the two bunny assets, the buttons, loading bar, and backgrounds"
  - title: A/B Test Results
    content: |
      It was interesting to see how people approached the two buttons. The immediate response was cuteness overload, but then decision paralysis on which button to press first.

      The tests were done with a small-sample size. As for the test A/B results, pink was selected more than blue, and feed was selected more than pet. I think a combination of factors are at play, and that left to right seems the most valuable placement for primary and secondary, and pink reads more as primary in these tests.
